## 1. Introduction

This document contains the agenda and minutes of the 5th preparation meeting of the Focus Group on Trust and Identity for Humans and Agentic AI (FG-TIDA), held during the "Interregnum" period: the phase between the launch on 9 July 2026 at the AI for Good Global Summit and the first meeting in Paris on 30 November – 1 December 2026. During this phase, the structure, leadership, and initial deliverables of the Focus Group are prepared, for approval at the Paris meeting.

With more than 45 theme proposals received, the leadership had consolidated part of them into three provisional Working Groups, presented at this meeting by their proposed chairs. Their creation, charters and chairs are to be confirmed at the Paris meeting; further Working Groups are being prepared for Paris and Geneva. The meeting also introduced the working methods for contributions, which open after this meeting.

### 5.2 Provisional Working Groups

The Chair recalled that more than 45 themes and proposals had been received and that part of them had been consolidated into three provisional Working Groups, to be finalized and approved in Paris. Working Group 0 serves all the other Working Groups, since use cases and a common taxonomy apply to all of them.

#### 5.2.1 WG0 — Use Cases, Taxonomy and Terminology (UCT) (Trisha Chakraborty, Cognizant) — [charter](https://fg-tida-wg0.github.io/wg0-uct-charter/)

The working name is UCT (use cases and terminology), and may change. The Working Group has two purposes:

- **Use cases.** A repository of scenarios across the landscape of agentic identity and trust, including hypothetical ones, open to any participant. Use cases are tagged to the relevant themes or Working Groups; those not yet matching a Working Group stay untagged until the Working Groups mature. WG0 decides only on the tagging; whether a use case is accepted is a decision of the Working Group concerned.
- **Terminology.** A single glossary to which every Working Group refers, instead of each Working Group creating its own definitions. Existing terms are reused where possible; WG0 decides which term holds for the Focus Group when definitions conflict or new definitions are needed. WG0 does not change technical specifications.

The proposed deliverables are a glossary of terms (technical specification), the use-case repository (migrated from the existing FG-TIDA repository, as an ongoing process), an analysis chart of use cases and themes per Working Group, and a review and mapping of use cases to Working Groups (work in progress).

#### 5.2.2 WG1 — Continuous and Dynamic Remote Attestation for Agentic AI (CDRA) (Houda Labiod, Huawei; Henk Birkholz, Fraunhofer SIT) — [charter](https://fg-tida-wg1.github.io/wg1-cdra-charter/)

As screen sharing failed, Debora Comparin displayed the slides. Following the comments received since the previous presentation, the Working Group is renamed "Continuous and Dynamic Remote Attestation for Agentic AI".

Following the remote attestation procedures terminology, an attester produces evidence about its state and a verifier appraises it and issues an attestation result. Remote attestation today covers hardware, environments and platforms, with snapshot evidence checked once at start: it shows what code runs where, but not the context or intent of an agent. An agent is a dynamic composite of model, code, tools, policy, context, data and memory, all of which can change at runtime and expand the trust surface. The Working Group is organized around three questions: what to attest (and what to measure), how to attest (layered appraisal, delegation between agents, legacy services that cannot produce or read evidence), and when to attest (continuous strategies).

The goal is pre-standardization work towards open, interoperable and privacy-preserving remote attestation of AI agents, reusing existing specifications. Proposed deliverables:

1. White paper on remote attestation challenges for agentic systems and gap analysis (technical report).
2. Requirements for continuous and dynamic remote attestation, including what to measure and when to refresh (technical specification).
3. Multi-layer attestation architecture framework, with data and interaction models (technical specification).
4. Integration of remote attestation into agent interaction protocols (technical specification), subject to confirmation by the FG-TIDA management.
5. Interoperability model including legacy systems (technical specification).
6. Technical governance of trust domains with changes in composition (technical report).

Out of scope: complete protocols, new languages, message or document formats; governance other than the technical governance of trust domains (no liability or jurisdiction); decisions on individual agent actions or on the validity of authorizations (the attestation result is an input to such decisions); agent identifiers, new hardware and training-data provenance. Deliverables may be merged in Paris; liaisons are foreseen with WG0, WG2, ITU-T, the IETF (RATS) and the Trusted Computing Group, and six related themes were identified for discussion with their proposers. Henk Birkholz added that the deliverables are proposals, to be refined by consensus, and that SG17 is not the only possible target.

Discussion:

- **Paolo Campegiani** raised intermittent connectivity, where attestation cannot be checked against a policy source (a use case he contributed on geographically dispersed systems), and asked whether "bi-weekly" meant every two weeks; the chairs confirmed one meeting every two weeks and that intermittent connectivity is part of the continuous attestation problem.
- **Islam Gouda** asked on which universal standards attestation would be based. Henk Birkholz named RFC 9334 (architecture), the Trusted Computing Group attestation frameworks and GlobalPlatform (mobile devices), with academia for runtime evidence from in-memory state — about 80% standards and 20% academia.
- **King Yew Choo** (True Primary) asked whether deliverable 4 would cover payment protocols, where a seller relying on an attestation result before starting paid work is a relying party (use case 35). Henk Birkholz distinguished payments made by an agent from the cost of agentic systems spawning without limits, and noted that only what can be evidenced can be attested; the use case should be refined for the use case document.
- **Jon-Rav Shende** (Thales) suggested a reference use case with a delegated agent executing a high-value transaction across trust domains, and focusing on which claims can be supported by trustworthy evidence and how relying services consume them without confusing attestation with authorization. Henk Birkholz agreed that delegation is central and mentioned emerging IETF drafts (OAuth working group) on authority delegation, to be discussed at IETF 127.

#### 5.2.3 WG2 — Identity, Behaviour and Enforcement for AI Agents (IBEA) (Artur Hecker, Huawei; Cyril Gorlla, CTGT) — [charter](https://fg-tida-wg2.github.io/wg2-ibea-charter/)

Presented from GitHub, without slides; a PDF of the charter is available. The Working Group builds on themes #4 and #6. **Artur Hecker** presented the problem: an AI agent's identity can be strongly bound, but, unlike deterministic software, says little about its behaviour. Security policies built on the access-control matrix grant rights to subjects; an agent with granted rights can still act in unexpected ways, and the same agent may act differently on the same request depending on configuration, context, tools or model. Blocking non-deterministic behaviour or revoking rights would defeat the purpose of agents, which are deployed precisely for situations that cannot be foreseen. Two solution paths are currently considered (identity-based and intent-based policy); others may be added by the community.

**Cyril Gorlla** presented the scope: WG1 attests, WG2 decides. The unit of work is the enforcement decision on one action, at the moment it is attempted, against the policy in force; sequences and delegation chains are series of such decisions. A decision has five possible outcomes — permit, remediate, block, escalate or indeterminate — and a statement that no check was made is kept apart and never read as permission (for example, a site cut off yields an indeterminate result). WG2 states requirements only, not encodings, schemas, wire formats or protocols. Proposed deliverables: (1) technical report on the problem and requirements; (2) technical specification of enforcement requirements; (3) and (4) reports on the identity path and the intent-based policy path, each with a study and a requirements part; (5) shared test fixtures, so that all paths are assessed against the same requirements and cases. Milestones over about one year: charter and outline of deliverable 1 in Paris, first draft in Geneva in January 2027, requirements at about six months, enforcement requirements and fixtures at about nine months, solution reports at the end. WG2 will work out its interface with WG1 in Paris, send new terms to WG0, follow the IETF authority-delegation work, and complement the SG17 work items X.dmst, XSTR.magAI-behavior and X.aplm-aia. The proposers of themes #26, #31, #47 and #49 have been invited to contribute.

Discussion:

- **Islam Gouda** saw an overlap with his work on a "humanized AI economy" framework, bringing the human and organizational perspective: who grants authority, how human interests are protected, who remains accountable and how meaningful oversight is maintained.
- **Duncan Sparrell** suggested a more explicit out-of-scope section, then noted it was already in the charter.
- Asked how to reach the chairs, Artur Hecker pointed to the Working Group mailing list on the FG-TIDA GitHub page; **Henk Birkholz** suggested opening GitHub issues on the charter, and the chairs accepted both. **Thomas Basikolo** explained that the Working Group mailing lists are being set up with the provisional chairs and that participants need to subscribe; emails may not reach the chairs for a few days.
- **Gianpaolo Scalone** (Vodafone) asked about the proposed Working Group on child age-aware identity and delegated authority for agentic AI, and offered to chair it. **Debora Comparin** said his presentation had raised strong interest and that the management is identifying the other provisional Working Groups for Paris; only three were presented at this call for lack of time. The Chair added that with more than 50 proposals, five or six Working Groups is a reasonable number, and encouraged proposers to join forces. Answering **Arnaud Taddei**, Gianpaolo Scalone confirmed that both his proposals — child online protection and a Working Group for operators — should be considered.

### 5.3 Tools and working methods

**Debora Comparin** (Co-Chair) presented, briefly for lack of time, the updated main page of the [FG-TIDA GitHub](https://github.com/FG-TIDA): the brainstorming and themes, the established Working Groups with links to their GitHub organizations (charters and how to contribute), and a "Contributing to TIDA" document (work in progress) describing the lifecycle of a work item. There are two cases: contributing to an existing work item (five steps, through GitHub or the GitLab mirror), or proposing a new work item, either within an existing Working Group or not yet attached to any. New work items are submitted through the [TIDA Data Tracker](https://tida-datatracker.org/), the open-source IETF Datatracker customized for FG-TIDA and synchronized automatically with GitHub and GitLab; submissions use an RST template and require an account. A video explaining the process will be recorded and shared the following week; feedback is welcome. The Chair added that a dedicated session in Paris or before could be held if needed.

The Chair recalled that the first meeting will be held at UNESCO in Paris on 30 November – 1 December 2026; registration through the online form is mandatory, by 20 November 2026, for logistics and for UNESCO security. The provisional agenda is published on the TIDA Data Tracker: day 1 with the mandate and objectives, WG1, WG2 and sessions in which selected use cases are presented by their proposers; day 2 with a further Working Group still to be identified, terminology and use cases (WG0), and an overview of feedback. Not all Working Groups will be finalized in Paris; some will be in Geneva. Documents, slides and recordings will be added to the Data Tracker.
